\begin{abstract}
\new{Home-cage monitoring (HCM) continuously observes laboratory animals in their living environment. Differences in sensing technologies, measurements, data formats, and the reporting of experimental context make these data difficult to compare and reuse. We present the Home-Cage Monitoring Ontology (HCMO), developed from the COST TEATIME HCM Definition to describe HCM as an integrated experimental and data-acquisition system. Its five modules cover enclosures, biological subjects, environmental conditions, observations and results, and acquisition technology. By distinguishing sensors, observations, and results, HCMO preserves the links between measurements, the devices that produce them, and their experimental context. The ontology selectively reuses established biomedical and Semantic Web standards and is openly available with reproducible machine-readable distributions, persistent identifiers, documentation, and executable validation resources. We evaluate its consistency, FAIRness, and interoperability through ontology-quality assessments, automated validation, and an ISA/RO-Crate conversion use case. HCMO provides a shared model for annotating and validating HCM metadata, supporting the comparison and reuse of data across monitoring platforms.}

\end{abstract}
