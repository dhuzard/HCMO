\begin{abstract}
\new{Home-cage monitoring (HCM) continuously observes laboratory animals in their living environment, providing rich behavioural and experimental data. However, differences in sensors, measurements, and data formats make these data difficult to compare and reuse, particularly when experimental context is poorly documented. We introduce the Home-Cage Monitoring Ontology (HCMO), based on the COST TEATIME HCM Definition, to describe HCM as an integrated experimental and data-acquisition system. Its five modules cover enclosures, biological subjects, environmental conditions, observations and results, and acquisition technology. By distinguishing sensors, observations, and results, HCMO preserves the links between devices and measured data. Version 0.4.1 selectively reuses established standards, including BFO, IAO, SOSA/SSN (2017), Schema.org, OWL-Time, QUDT, and SemTS. HCMO is openly released with reproducible machine-readable distributions, SHACL validation shapes, competency questions, persistent identifiers, and documentation. We evaluate its consistency, FAIRness, and interoperability through automated validation, ontology-quality assessments, and an ISA/RO-Crate conversion fixture. The HermiT reasoner finds no inconsistent class, and twelve SPARQL competency questions return exactly their expected answers.}

\keywords{ontology \and home-cage monitoring \and laboratory animals \and FAIR data \and SOSA/SSN \and 3Rs}
\end{abstract}
