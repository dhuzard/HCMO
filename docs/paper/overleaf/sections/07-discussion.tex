\section{Discussion, limitations, and future work}
\new{The evaluation confirms that HCMO is consistent and executable, with FAIR scoring and automated checks of its shapes, competency questions, and test fixtures. However, formal alignments, ISA conformance, and community adoption remain to be demonstrated.}

\textbf{Limitations.} \new{HCMO remains an evolving resource, with some distinctions still being refined, particularly between raw data, processed measurements, observations, and biological results. The diversity of HCM systems also makes it difficult to balance broad applicability with experimental precision. For example, measurements may be collected at the cage, group, or individual level, complicating the definition of experimental units and their consistent representation in ISA. Alignment with external vocabularies remains partial: the ISA/OBI/STATO example demonstrates interoperability at the instance level, not formal class mappings. Similarly, the factorial design and mixed-model examples serve as interoperability tests rather than a comprehensive statistical framework.}

\textbf{Future work.} Planned directions include expert review of definitions and axioms identified by the class and property audits; systematic alignment decisions (reuse as-is vs specialise vs keep HCMO-specific) and bridge modules to adjacent ontologies (e.g.\ OBI, STATO, MEDO)\new{, following the optional BFO/SOSA profile} and to taxa/anatomy/device vocabularies; extending the current selective QUDT pattern to additional quantity roles where justified; and populating the ontology with real datasets via the TEATIME network and publishing the corresponding SPARQL queries. HCMO is also designed to underpin tooling that is not a contribution of this paper: \emph{knowledge-graph question answering} with ontology-checked queries \cite{sanou2026,bian2025,butz}; \emph{assisted authoring} through forms generated from the ontology and its shapes; and \emph{vendor-data mapping} of proprietary exports into HCMO with preserved provenance.


\textbf{Conclusion.} \new{To our knowledge, HCMO is the first ontology to describe home-cage monitoring as an integrated system linking animals, housing, environmental conditions, and data acquisition. Developed within the COST TEATIME community, it provides a version-pinned, validated, and citable resource. With further development and community adoption, HCMO aims to provide a common foundation for annotating, comparing, and reusing HCM data across laboratories.}
