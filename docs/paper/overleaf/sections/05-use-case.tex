\section{Interoperability use case}
\new{HCM study metadata travel through exchange formats such as ISA \cite{isa}, and their statistics are described with OBI and STATO. We separate the \emph{TBox} (HCMO's class and property axioms, where OBI and STATO are neither imported nor aligned), the \emph{ABox} (instance and evidence graphs, where OBI, STATO, and ISA identifiers occur), and the \emph{CBox} (HCMO's SKOS vocabularies, reached only through properties with range \nolinkurl{skos:Concept}). The release shows at the ABox level how HCMO data connect to the OBI/STATO ecosystem, in two steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence-layers}).}

\textbf{A recording-to-result evidence slice.} A pinned example, \texttt{examples/isa-hcmo-bridge.ttl}\fnurl{https://github.com/dhuzard/HCMO/tree/v0.4.1/examples}, uses PROV-O, specific OBI and STATO types, and ISA/Bioschemas exchange terms together with HCMO in one acyclic workflow from a raw recording to a statistical result \cite{isa,rocrate}. \new{The recording is an OBI \emph{assay}, the summary an OBI \emph{data transformation}; the HCMO study factor, its value, the experimental group, and the daily mean are also typed as OBI \emph{study design independent variable} and STATO \emph{factor level}, \emph{study group population}, and \emph{sample mean}.} This is validated interoperability evidence, not an HCMO class mapping or a claim of formal ISA RO-Crate conformance.

\textbf{A 2 $\times$ 2 factorial study with repeated measures.} \new{The second step generates a study of eight individually housed animals, crossing treatment (vehicle or active) with cage enrichment (standard or enriched) in four groups of two. Dark-phase activity is recorded over seven days (56 observations), with day as a within-animal repeated measure rather than a third factor. One animal is re-housed during the study. This minimal factorial design represents two crossed factors and their interaction, while repeated measures and re-housing test temporal links between animals, observations, and enclosures. The fixture assesses whether HCMO--ISA conversion preserves animal identity, housing history, factors, groups, repeated observations, a derived tissue sample, and statistical results, identifying any information loss. Supplementary Fig.~S1 presents a Hasse diagram distinguishing the animal (experimental unit) from the animal-day (observational unit)\fnurl{https://github.com/dhuzard/HCMO/blob/main/docs/paper/supplementary/hasse-2x2-design.pdf}.}

\new{The fixture also tests how HCMO handles changes over time. One animal moves between enclosures without overlapping housing assignments, and its dark-phase observations remain linked to the correct enclosure. The example includes a derived tissue sample (\nolinkurl{bioschemas:Sample}) and a mixed model fitted to the generated data using pinned numerical dependencies. The model, its estimates, confidence intervals, and p-values are represented separately, alongside file entities (\nolinkurl{schema:MediaObject}) and exact row fragments. The model serialisation and the active-versus-vehicle contrast at standard enrichment link to distinct result rows. All files are available in \nolinkurl{examples/isa-roundtrip/}: the input data (\nolinkurl{dark-phase-activity.csv}), STATO-annotated results (\nolinkurl{model-results.csv}), RDF representation (\nolinkurl{canonical.ttl}), and documentation (\nolinkurl{README.md}).}

\textbf{Three projections, two levels of fidelity.} Canonical HCMO RDF and an extended ISA RO-Crate JSON-LD serialisation are graph-isomorphic at 1,539 triples. \nolinkurl{roc-validator} 0.11.3 passes all RO-Crate 1.2 required rules and all ISA-specific required rules; its full inherited ISA run has one isolated upstream conflict, since the embedded ISA profile requires RO-Crate 1.1 although its minimal ISA fixture uses the 1.2 context. Formal ISA conformance therefore remains deferred: the draft profile has no permanent URI and its validator disagrees about the base RO-Crate edition.

\new{In contrast, native ISA-JSON and ISA-Tab preserve only part of the information. Using \nolinkurl{isatools} 0.14.3, we generated both formats and validated their JSON--Tab--JSON round trip. The animal Source, derived tissue Sample, collection process, and HCMO identifiers (retained as ISA comments) survive conversion. However, housing history, whole-animal recordings, factor assignments, experimental groups, repeated observations, and semantic STATO/file-fragment links cannot be fully preserved. Rather than introducing artificial Sample entities, these losses are documented and verified against loss manifests (Fig.~\ref{fig:roundtrip}). Generated files and modelling decisions are available in \nolinkurl{native-isa/}.}
