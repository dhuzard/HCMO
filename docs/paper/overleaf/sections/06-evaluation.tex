\section{Evaluation}
HCMO is evaluated at five complementary levels: coverage of real HCM systems by mapping their native exports to instance graphs, executable competency questions over those instance graphs, common ontology pitfalls and FAIR metadata, OWL consistency with structural metrics, and SHACL validation. Every artifact behind the numbers below (mapping tables, generators, instance graphs, queries with complete expected answers, and generated reports) is versioned under \nolinkurl{evaluation/} and \nolinkurl{docs/hcm-systems/systems/} in the repository and is regenerated deterministically by the CI gate, so that historical scores cannot be silently attributed to a later artifact.

\textbf{Coverage of heterogeneous HCM systems.} Six systems spanning the heterogeneity HCMO claims to absorb were annotated (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}): a commercial capacitive cage-rack system (Tecniplast DVC), a commercial metabolic and environmental system (TSE PhenoMaster), a commercial video system with behaviour recognition (Noldus PhenoTyper with EthoVision XT), an open-source group-housing depth-video and RFID tracker (Live Mouse Tracker), and two open-source operant devices, one for feeding (FED3) and one for home-cage touch-screen cognitive testing (BEATBox). For each system we documented sensors, raw outputs, derived variables and file formats, wrote a reviewed mapping table from every native concept or export variable to HCMO classes and properties, and generated an instance graph from a native export with a deterministic script. The DVC graph derives from a \emph{real} contributed export (cohort 7623: 24 cages, one rack, 14 days); all 24 cages, their real insertion and removal events and 960 real one-minute activation-index bins are materialised, together with a synthetic second cohort of female cages and the experimental metadata that the export omits (light programme, rack temperature and humidity, two animals per cage, weekly cage changes), each linked to a dataset node that marks it as an assumption rather than a measurement. No other vendor export could be redistributed, so the five remaining graphs derive from synthetic exports that reproduce the documented column layout of each system: the PhenoMaster table export, the EthoVision track, time-bin and behaviour exports, the FED3 SD-card log, the four SQLite tables that the \nolinkurl{lmt-analysis} package reads (with its exact event vocabulary), and the BEATBox inter-module CAN messages as published in its manual. The mapping is therefore exercised faithfully even though the values are generated, and every graph states its provenance. Each graph conforms to the standard shapes with ontology-aware RDFS entailment.

Figure~\ref{fig:dvcchain} shows the sensor $\rightarrow$ observation $\rightarrow$ result chain for one real DVC bin. \emph{Lessons learned.} The mapping exposed where the heterogeneity is absorbed and where it is not, and the ordering of the last column of Table~\ref{tab:coverage} is itself the finding. (i) Enclosure, housing, environment, sensor, actuator, software and file provenance mapped natively in all six systems, including the real DVC rack events as time-bounded operational status records, the EthoVision and LMT tracks as SemTS location tables, and every BEATBox module as the sensor, actuator or controller it is. (ii) HCMO 0.3.0 has observation subtypes for behaviour, weight, environment and gas concentration, but none for a numeric activity, metabolic or operant result about an animal or a cage group; such results (DVC index, VO\textsubscript{2}, distance moved, retrieval latency, response latency) were expressed as generic \nolinkurl{sosa:Observation}s with \nolinkurl{hcm-obs:QuantityValue} results. (iii) The two systems added last score lowest on native coverage, and for two distinct structural reasons. LMT records \emph{n-ary social events} with up to four participants, while the behaviour shape admits exactly one feature of interest: an event is therefore stored as one shared \nolinkurl{BehaviorResult} observed about each participant, which keeps every participant queryable (CQ-MS-15 recovers the group size) but loses the participant \emph{roles}, and genotype has to be smuggled in as group membership. BEATBox exposes the gap that a \emph{task, trial and session} vocabulary is missing entirely: a trial survives only as a set of observations sharing an interval, and the task and its schedule are free text on a reused procedure. (iv) Cage-level systems remain partially attributable: the DVC export identifies cages, not animals, so the feature of interest stays a cage-level group even once the individual animals are known, and rack positions and arena zones have no history because \nolinkurl{hcm:locatedIn} is timeless. These gaps are the input to the next modelling round rather than silent extensions: no term was added to the 0.3.0 release to make a mapping fit.

\textbf{Competency questions on instance data.} Sixteen cross-system questions were executed with rdflib over the union of the ontology and the six instance graphs (64\,444 triples); each is versioned with its complete expected answer rows and checked by the CI gate (Table~\ref{tab:cqms}). Fourteen join data from several systems, for example ``which animals or cage groups, across systems, were observed in enriched enclosures during the dark phase of their light cycle'' (answerable for all six systems, and for the DVC only because the light programme is supplied as declared metadata), ``which behaviours involve more than one animal at the same time'' (which recovers LMT group sizes of two, three and four), or ``how many trials did each strain run on a touch-screen task and how accurate were they''. One question is reported as \emph{partial}: the derivation-procedure question returns rows for five systems and, correctly, none for the DVC, whose index is computed by proprietary undocumented software, so only file-level attribution is available. One question (results without units) has a verified empty answer.

The eleven canonical release questions continue to run over the ontology plus the positive examples, and five further questions over the isolated 2 $\times$ 2 round-trip fixture; the validator checks their complete expected answer rows, including the intentionally empty missing-dimensions answer, so an unexamined empty result is a validation failure.

\textbf{Ontology metrics and formal checks.} Table~\ref{tab:metrics} summarises the release. Every class and property in the five domain modules carries a label and a textual definition. HermiT (via owlready2) loads 65 classes from the canonical RDF/XML artifact, reports zero unsatisfiable classes, and classification adds no named subclass edge beyond the 73 asserted ones: the inferred hierarchy equals the asserted one. OOPS! \cite{oops2012} rerun on the immutable 0.3.0 artifact reports P04, P08, P10, P13, P22, P34 and P35; P34/P35 concern reused SOSA, SemTS, QUDT, Schema.org and PROV terms whose authoritative declarations are governed by the pinned external-vocabulary contract rather than copied into the graph, P10 is the scanner's generic missing-disjointness heuristic naming no pair, and P13 marks 32 properties without inverses that are not asserted without definition-level evidence; no finding demonstrates an inconsistency. FOOPS! \cite{foops} scores 1.0 on the same artifact, and AskWol reported no contradiction or unsatisfiable class; raw responses and triage are archived under \nolinkurl{docs/paper/evaluation/}.

\textbf{SHACL validation.} The validator supplies the canonical ontology as a separate ontology graph and enables RDFS entailment, so that domain, range and subclass consequences select the same \nolinkurl{sh:targetClass} constraints as explicit types. On the six system graphs (63\,047 triples) the shapes produce zero violations, i.e.\ no false positive, while twenty-four injected faults (a unit removed from a quantity, a reversed housing interval, an overlapping second assignment, an enclosure without sensor, on each graph) are all rejected; the intentionally invalid release fixtures produce 18 and 5 violations. One negative fixture deliberately omits explicit \nolinkurl{rdf:type}: it conforms without the ontology and fails with ontology-aware inference, guarding the entailment contract. Running the shapes against the instance graphs also revealed that the constraint rejecting deprecated timeless booleans used a \nolinkurl{VALUES} clause, which the SHACL specification forbids in SPARQL constraints; pySHACL had been reporting a validation failure that the gate counted as non-conformance. The constraint was rewritten with an equivalent \nolinkurl{FILTER}, and the fixture now fails with genuine violations.

The accepted round-trip fixture contains a 2 $\times$ 2 treatment-by-enrichment design with eight individually housed animals, 56 repeated dark-phase activity observations, non-overlapping re-housing, an actual derived tissue Sample, and separate fitted-model, estimate, confidence-interval, p-value, File, and file-fragment entities. Canonical HCMO RDF and extended ISA RO-Crate JSON-LD are graph-isomorphic at 1,539 triples. Dedicated shapes and injected probes reject overlapping, orphaned, reversed, or incompletely generated housing assignments; generated replacement animals; observation/housing mismatch; group/factor disagreement; and fabricated ISA assignment results. \nolinkurl{roc-validator} 0.11.3 passes all RO-Crate 1.2 required rules and all ISA-specific required rules, and pinned \nolinkurl{isatools} 0.14.3 validates an executable native ISA-JSON/ISA-Tab projection whose controlled losses are reported explicitly; we report this as interoperability evidence, not formal conformance.
