\section{Evaluation}
HCMO is evaluated at five complementary levels: coverage of real HCM systems by mapping their native exports to instance graphs, executable competency questions over those instance graphs, common ontology pitfalls and FAIR metadata, OWL consistency with structural metrics, and SHACL validation. Every artifact behind the numbers below (mapping tables, generators, instance graphs, queries with complete expected answers, and generated reports) is versioned under \nolinkurl{evaluation/} and \nolinkurl{docs/hcm-systems/systems/} in the repository and is regenerated deterministically by the CI gate, so that historical scores cannot be silently attributed to a later artifact.

\textbf{Coverage of heterogeneous HCM systems.} Four systems spanning the heterogeneity HCMO claims to absorb were annotated (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}): a commercial capacitive cage-rack system (Tecniplast DVC), a commercial metabolic and environmental system (TSE PhenoMaster), a commercial video system with behaviour recognition (Noldus PhenoTyper with EthoVision XT), and an open-source operant feeding device placed inside a standard cage (FED3). For each system we documented sensors, raw outputs, derived variables and file formats, wrote a reviewed mapping table from every native concept or export variable to HCMO classes and properties, and generated an instance graph from a native export with a deterministic script. The DVC graph derives from a \emph{real} contributed export (cohort 7623: 24 cages, one rack, 14 days); all 24 cages, their real insertion and removal events and 720 real one-minute activation-index bins (four cages over three hours) are materialised. No other vendor export could be redistributed, so the three remaining graphs derive from synthetic exports that reproduce the documented column layout of each system (PhenoMaster table export, EthoVision track, time-bin and behaviour exports, FED3 SD-card log); the mapping is therefore exercised faithfully even though the values are generated, and every graph states its provenance. Each graph conforms to the standard shapes with ontology-aware RDFS entailment.

Figure~\ref{fig:dvcchain} shows the sensor $\rightarrow$ observation $\rightarrow$ result chain for one real DVC bin. \emph{Lessons learned.} The mapping exposed where the heterogeneity is absorbed and where it is not. (i) Enclosure, housing, environment, sensor, actuator, software and file provenance mapped natively in all four systems, including the real DVC rack events as time-bounded operational status records and the EthoVision track as a SemTS location table. (ii) HCMO 0.3.0 has observation subtypes for behaviour, weight, environment and gas concentration, but none for a numeric activity, metabolic or operant result about an animal or a cage group; such results (DVC index, VO\textsubscript{2}, distance moved, retrieval latency) were expressed as generic \nolinkurl{sosa:Observation}s with \nolinkurl{hcm-obs:QuantityValue} results, which is why the obs column carries most partial mappings and why the native share stays near 50\%. (iii) The DVC export identifies cages, not animals: its feature of interest had to be a cage-level group, strain and sex cannot be asserted on a group, and the rack position has no history because \nolinkurl{hcm:locatedIn} is timeless. (iv) The DVC export carries neither enrichment nor light schedule, so environment-dependent questions are unanswerable for DVC \emph{because of the export}, not the ontology; the cohort aggregates, device lifecycle events and reference or telemetry channels were deliberately left out of scope. These gaps are the input to the next modelling round rather than silent extensions: no term was added to the 0.3.0 release to make a mapping fit.

\textbf{Competency questions on instance data.} Fourteen cross-system questions were executed with rdflib over the union of the ontology and the four instance graphs (36\,648 triples); each is versioned with its complete expected answer rows and checked by the CI gate (Table~\ref{tab:cqms}). Ten questions join data from several systems, for example ``which animals or cage groups, across systems, were observed in enriched enclosures during the dark phase of their light cycle'' (derived from \nolinkurl{hcm-env:LightCycle}) or ``how many subjects or cage groups were housed in each system at two reference times''. Two are reported as \emph{partial}: the dark-phase and the procedure-provenance questions return rows for three systems and, correctly, none for DVC, whose export states no light schedule and whose index derivation is proprietary. One question (results without units) has a verified empty answer.

\textbf{Ontology metrics and formal checks.} Table~\ref{tab:metrics} summarises the release. Every class and property in the five domain modules carries a label and a textual definition. HermiT (via owlready2) loads 65 classes from the canonical RDF/XML artifact, reports zero unsatisfiable classes, and classification adds no named subclass edge beyond the 73 asserted ones: the inferred hierarchy equals the asserted one. OOPS! \cite{oops2012} rerun on the immutable 0.3.0 artifact reports P04, P08, P10, P13, P22, P34 and P35; P34/P35 concern reused SOSA, SemTS, QUDT, Schema.org and PROV terms whose authoritative declarations are governed by the pinned external-vocabulary contract rather than copied into the graph, P10 is the scanner's generic missing-disjointness heuristic naming no pair, and P13 marks 32 properties without inverses that are not asserted without definition-level evidence; no finding demonstrates an inconsistency. FOOPS! \cite{foops} scores 1.0 on the same artifact, and AskWol reported no contradiction or unsatisfiable class; raw responses and triage are archived under \nolinkurl{docs/paper/evaluation/}.

\textbf{SHACL validation.} The validator supplies the canonical ontology as a separate ontology graph and enables RDFS entailment, so that domain, range and subclass consequences select the same \nolinkurl{sh:targetClass} constraints as explicit types. On the four system graphs (35\,251 triples) the shapes produce zero violations, i.e.\ no false positive, while sixteen injected faults (a unit removed from a quantity, a reversed housing interval, an overlapping second assignment, an enclosure without sensor, on each graph) are all rejected; the intentionally invalid release fixtures produce 18 and 5 violations. One negative fixture deliberately omits explicit \nolinkurl{rdf:type}: it conforms without the ontology and fails with ontology-aware inference, guarding the entailment contract. Running the shapes against the instance graphs also revealed that the constraint rejecting deprecated timeless booleans used a \nolinkurl{VALUES} clause, which the SHACL specification forbids in SPARQL constraints; pySHACL had been reporting a validation failure that the gate counted as non-conformance. The constraint was rewritten with an equivalent \nolinkurl{FILTER}, and the fixture now fails with genuine violations.

The accepted round-trip fixture contains a 2 $\times$ 2 treatment-by-enrichment design with eight individually housed animals, 56 repeated dark-phase activity observations, non-overlapping re-housing, an actual derived tissue Sample, and separate fitted-model, estimate, confidence-interval, p-value, File, and file-fragment entities. Canonical HCMO RDF and extended ISA RO-Crate JSON-LD are graph-isomorphic at 1,539 triples. Dedicated shapes and injected probes reject overlapping, orphaned, reversed, or incompletely generated housing assignments; generated replacement animals; observation/housing mismatch; group/factor disagreement; and fabricated ISA assignment results. \nolinkurl{roc-validator} 0.11.3 passes all RO-Crate 1.2 required rules and all ISA-specific required rules, and pinned \nolinkurl{isatools} 0.14.3 validates an executable native ISA-JSON/ISA-Tab projection whose controlled losses are reported explicitly; we report this as interoperability evidence, not formal conformance.
